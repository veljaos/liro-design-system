## 1. decisions.md entries (ready to paste)

- **2026-10-08 — Sign-in building blocks (P5.6, with the owner's addition).** The flows live in the Core; the Design System gives the pieces inside `AuthShell` (shadcn's login-03), all exported.
  1. **`EmailFirstForm`** (shadcn's login-05 shape): the e-mail field (`type="email"`, `autocomplete="username"` so a password manager pairs it with the next step's password), a full-width primary **Continue** (ArrowRight, mirrored in right-to-left), then the "or" divider and the providers' buttons (`providers`; `providersPosition` 'after' by default as login-05, or 'before'), then optional lines `signUp` ("No account? …", sm `text.secondary`) and `terms` (xs `text.tertiary`), centred like the card's other small lines. `onSubmit` receives the trimmed address; the form has `noValidate` — the Core checks the address and returns `error`. `loading` keeps Continue's size and name, hides its content behind the 20px oval loader (ConfirmDialog's look), `aria-busy`, a second press does nothing; `disabled` with `disabledReason` shows the reason under the field and makes every button unavailable. Label and button default to `messages['signIn.email' | 'signIn.continue']` ("E-mail", "Continue"); the example passes "Work e-mail".
  2. **`ProviderSignInButtons`**: "Continue with Microsoft" / "Continue with Google" — providers and labels from the application (`id`, `label`, `mark`, `onClick`, `disabled`, `loading`). **Neutral default buttons** (raised, `border.default`), full width, 8px apart, the label as given; **never the provider's colour as a fill**. The mark is an `<img>` of the address the application passes (decorative, `alt=""`: the label names the provider), 18px, 12px before the label (Microsoft's spacing). The pressed provider is busy (loader, `aria-busy`); the others are `aria-disabled` until the redirect. `divider` 'above' / 'below' draws the "or" divider (`messages['signIn.or']`, xs `text.secondary` between two 1px `border.default` lines).
  3. **Provider marks (D18: no component contains a logo).** The official marks are files of `@veljaos/tokens`: `brand/providers/microsoft.svg` (Microsoft's four squares as its "Sign in with Microsoft" guidelines give them: 21 × 21, four 9px squares 1px apart, #F25022, #7FBA00, #00A4EF, #FFB900) and `brand/providers/google.svg` (Google's standard-colour "G" from its Sign in with Google branding guidelines, 48 × 48 viewBox, #EA4335, #4285F4, #FBBC05, #34A853). Both are exported through the existing `./brand/*` export and the package's `files`. `brand.test.ts` lists them and checks each: a viewBox, a `<title>` with the provider's name, no text, images, fonts or scripts, and exactly the provider's colours in order. Both providers allow their colour mark on a light and on a dark neutral button (Microsoft's dark button #2F2F2F, Google's dark button #131314 with the colour "G"), so one file each serves both themes. **Trademarks:** Microsoft and the Microsoft logo are trademarks of the Microsoft group of companies; Google and the "G" logo are trademarks of Google LLC. They are shipped only so an application can show the provider's sign-in button as the provider requires; they are not covered by this repository's licence (see "Third-party" below). The labels follow the providers' wording ("Continue with …" / "Sign in with …"); the font is ours (the providers' rules on the label font — Segoe UI, Roboto — are for their own button images; an application that must match them exactly can pass its own button).
  4. **`PasswordField`**: paste always allowed (WCAG 3.3.8), `autocomplete` 'current-password' (default) or 'new-password', `autocapitalize`/`autocorrect`/`spellcheck` off, written left to right in every language (a password is a code), aligned to the field's start. The show/hide toggle is a 28px subtle compact button inside the field at its end (the input keeps 40px at its end), named `messages['password.show']` ("Show password") with `aria-pressed` (pressed = shown) and `aria-controls`; Eye / EyeOff; a pointer press keeps the caret in the field (mousedown prevented). While Caps Lock is on (read from the key events' modifier state) a hint "Caps Lock is on" (`messages['password.capsLock']`) stands under the field in a polite status region and joins the field's description. States through Field: description, error, required, disabled with reason, read-only (no toggle).
  5. **`CodeInput`** (one-time codes): one `role="group"` named by the label, its boxes text inputs named "Character 2 of 6" (`messages['code.box'](position, positionText, total, totalText)`, numbers through `format.number`), description, error (every box `aria-invalid`) and disabled reason as Field's. **Pasting the whole code works into any box**: a text with at least as many usable characters as there are boxes fills them all from the first (`fillCode`); a shorter text fills from the box pasted into; spaces and dashes are dropped ("482 913", "ABCD-EFGH"). The first box has `autocomplete="one-time-code"` (the phone offers the code from a message; the browser fills that box and the same rule spreads it); numeric codes have `inputMode="numeric"` and `pattern="[0-9]*"`; `kind="alphanumeric"` takes letters in upper case. Keys: a character fills its box and moves on; Backspace empties the box, or the one before when it is empty, and moves there (`eraseCode`); Delete empties the box; the arrows, Home and End move (`codeKeyTarget`). **Direction (choice):** a code is written left to right in every language, as numbers, money and codes are (P2.3a, P3.6c), so the boxes keep that order in a right-to-left page (`dir="ltr"` on the group) and stand at the page's start (the right); the arrows follow the boxes' own order — the code's leading edge is its left (B.7). Boxes 40 × 44px, 8px apart (six fit a 360px phone with 16px padding), the field's look, centred md text, tabular digits. `onChange` gives the value with an empty box as a space; `onComplete(code)` fires when every box is filled; `name` submits the code as one hidden value; `autoFocus` focuses the first box.
  6. **`RecoveryCodes`**: shown once — a warning Alert (`messages['recovery.shownOnce']`) above the codes; the codes in two columns from 36em (one on phones, `layout`), monospace, left to right, `select-all`, on `surface.sunken`, a list named `messages['recovery.codes']`. Three small neutral buttons: **Copy** (the codes one per line through the Clipboard API; "Copied" or, when the browser refuses, what to do instead, in a status region), **Download** (a text file made in the browser — `recoveryCodesText(heading, codes)`: the application's heading, an empty line, one code per line, "\n" line ends — through a Blob and a temporary link; nothing is fetched; `fileName`, default "recovery-codes.txt"), **Print** (the codes alone, printed from a hidden frame that is removed afterwards, not the whole page). `onAction('copy' | 'download' | 'print')` tells the application which way was used (its security log). "I have saved these codes" (a checkbox) enables **Continue** (primary, full width, last). `loading`: skeleton lines while the Core makes the codes, every action unavailable.
  7. **`SessionList`**: the devices signed in to the account, rows divided by `border.subtle` lines (no padding at the list's outer edges: the card the application gives it sets them, P4.9). Each: the device kind's 20px icon (Monitor, Smartphone, Tablet — it says which device), the device as the Core names it (sm semibold), a neutral "This device" badge on the current one (`aria-current`), and in xs `text.secondary` the place, the address (left to right) and "Active now" or "Last active 06.10.2026. 08:15" (`messages['session.lastActive'](when)`, the instant's own date and time through `instantText`). Other devices have a small neutral **Sign out** (LogOut, named "Sign out: Edge on Windows"), busy while the Core's promise runs; the current device has none (the user menu signs out). **`extraAction`** is a slot per row before Sign out for the application's "This wasn't me" (a link to its security flow). **"Sign out all other devices"** under the list (`onRevokeOthers`). Only this device: "No other device is signed in." `readOnly` (an administrator's view): no actions. `loading`: three skeleton rows.
  - **New helper `instantText(format, instant)`** (exported): an ISO instant in the tenant's offset written as `format.date` of its day and `format.time` of its clock ("05.10.2026. 14:12"), so day and time always agree (the P4.9 notifications rule), never shifted to the device's zone like `format.dateTime`. Used by SessionList and SignerList.
  - Messages: `signIn.email`, `signIn.continue`, `signIn.or`, `password.show`, `password.capsLock`, `code.box`, `recovery.codes`, `recovery.shownOnce`, `recovery.copy`, `recovery.copied`, `recovery.copyFailed`, `recovery.download`, `recovery.print`, `recovery.saved`, `recovery.continue`, `session.thisDevice`, `session.activeNow`, `session.lastActive`, `session.revoke`, `session.revokeNamed`, `session.revokeOthers`, `session.noOthers`.

- **2026-10-08 — KanbanBoard (P5.7).** `KanbanBoard` (exported) with `moveCard`, `placeOf`, `kanbanKeyTarget`, `dropTarget`, `dragLayout` (exported, tested in both directions).
  1. **Columns** 280px wide, 16px apart, from the leading edge; the board scrolls sideways inside its own region (named by `label`) when they do not fit. A column is an area on `surface.sunken`, radius lg, 8px padding — **not a card**: the cards are the only cards (P4.9 rule 4); its heading (sm semibold, h2 by default, `headingLevel`) and, at the end, the application's count with its noun ("3 tasks", `count`; the board never counts on its own, rule 2). An empty column says "No cards" (`messages['kanban.emptyColumn']`, xs `text.tertiary`) and is still a drop target (at least 80px high).
  2. **Cards** (raised, `border.default`, radius md, 12px padding, 8px apart): the title (sm medium; a link through `linkComponent` when the card has `href`, its colour named in every state), at most two description lines, the record it is about as "Invoice F-2026-0411 · Overdue" (P4.9 rule 7: kind, number, state; the link is **underlined** because it stands in a line of text — axe `link-in-text-block`, WCAG 1.4.1), then the due date (DueDate: the date always, its state as a small note), the application's meta ("2 comments") and the assignee's avatar (named) at the end.
  3. **Dragging (decisions "Launchpad dragging is live", the general rule):** pointer events, never HTML5 drag and drop; a mouse drag starts after 4px, a touch after a 250ms press without moving more than 8px (a swipe still scrolls; once a touch drag has started `touchmove` is cancelled). The card lifts — `shadow-lg`, `border.strong`, the raised layer, `cursor-grabbing` — and follows the pointer; the other cards slide to their new places with the slow motion token (250ms, the standard easing; 0ms under reduced motion through the token); a placeholder of the card's height, radius md, a 2px dashed `border.strong` on `surface.sunken` — neutral, never blue — marks where it lands. The place is measured on the laid-out board when the drag starts: the column under the card's centre (else the nearest), and in it the place after every other card whose centre is above the dragged card's centre (`dropTarget`); the other cards' shifts and the placeholder come from `dragLayout` (8px gap). Measured slots need no mirroring in right-to-left (B.7). Releasing reports the move; Escape or a cancelled pointer puts everything back. A target column that is shorter gets the placeholder's room at its bottom.
  4. **Keyboard (WCAG 2.5.7: every drag has another way):** each card has a 28px handle (GripVertical) named "Move: <title>" (`messages['kanban.moveCard']`) described by the instructions (`messages['kanban.instructions']`). **Space or Enter picks the card up** (`aria-pressed`), **ArrowUp/ArrowDown** move it within the column, the arrow pointing **forward in the reading direction** moves it to the next column (ArrowRight in left-to-right, ArrowLeft in right-to-left) keeping its height in the list as far as the other column allows (`kanbanKeyTarget`), **Space or Enter drops** it, **Escape cancels**; leaving the handle with Tab also cancels. The picked-up card is drawn lifted at its new place and the handle keeps the focus across columns. Every step is announced in a polite status region: "Picked up …. To do, position 1 of 3.", "…: In progress, position 2 of 3.", "Dropped …", "Move cancelled. … is back in its place." (`messages['kanban.lifted' | 'kanban.moved' | 'kanban.dropped' | 'kanban.cancelled']`, positions through `format.number`).
  5. **Menu** (MoreHorizontal, 28px, named "Move to: <title>"): "Move to" (a label) and the other columns — **the card goes to the end of that column (choice: it does not jump ahead of work already there)** —, then Move up / Move down (disabled at the ends). On phones it is the main way between columns.
  6. **Phones** (below 48em or `layout="phone"`): one column at a time, chosen in a SelectField "Column" whose options carry the counts ("In progress · 2 tasks"); `column` / `onColumnChange` make it controlled; a card moved by the keyboard to another column takes the select with it.
  7. **`onMove({ cardId, from, to })`**: the index is counted without the card itself, as `moveCard` applies it; the board shows what it is given, so the application decides (and may refuse). `readOnly`: no handles, menus or dragging. `loading`: skeleton columns.
  - Messages: `kanban.emptyColumn`, `kanban.moveCard`, `kanban.cardMenu`, `kanban.moveTo`, `kanban.moveToColumn`, `kanban.moveUp`, `kanban.moveDown`, `kanban.instructions`, `kanban.lifted`, `kanban.moved`, `kanban.dropped`, `kanban.cancelled`, `kanban.column`.

- **2026-10-08 — Users and roles (P5.21): PermissionMatrix, the rest composed.** `PermissionMatrix` (exported) with `isAllowed`, `setPermission`, `matrixKeyTarget`, `matrixFocusTarget` (exported, tested).
  1. **A table** (its caption the application's `label`): the areas as row headers (name sm semibold, an optional description xs `text.secondary`), the actions as column headers (xs semibold `text.secondary`, centred), a checkbox per cell named "Edit: Sales invoices" (`messages['permissions.cell'](area, action)`). The value is the application's (`area id → action ids`); `onChange(value, { area, action, allowed })` reports every change, the actions of an area kept in the columns' order.
  2. **A cell the action does not apply to** (`applies` false: reports are only viewed) shows a dash, "Not applicable" for assistive technology, and takes no focus. **A cell the Core does not allow this role** (`unavailable` returns the reason) keeps its checkbox, drawn disabled but **focusable** (`aria-disabled`, so the reason can be read): the reason is its description and its tooltip, and it cannot change.
  3. **Built-in roles are read-only** (`readOnly`): a check ("Allowed") or a dash ("Not allowed", "Not applicable") as text — never disabled checkboxes (P2.2: read-only is plain text).
  4. **Keyboard:** one checkbox in the tab order (roving); the arrows move from checkbox to checkbox, past cells that have none, the arrow pointing forward in the reading direction to the next column (B.7); Home and End to the row's first and last checkbox, Ctrl (Cmd) + Home / End to the matrix's first and last; Space changes the checkbox. **Choice:** a `table` with this keyboard model, not `role="grid"` — the repository's `jsx-a11y` rules forbid an interactive role on a table, and the table keeps its native row and column headers for screen readers.
  5. **Phones** (below 48em or `layout="phone"`): each area is a fieldset (its legend the area and its description) with its actions as CheckboxFields one under the other; unavailable actions are disabled with the reason under them; read-only ones as text.
  - **Roles, members and invitations are composed, not new components** (one pattern per problem): a DataTable of members (avatar, name and e-mail, roles, a status badge, the last sign-in through `instantText`, a row menu: Change roles, Deactivate), a DataTable of roles (built-in or custom, a count of members with its noun), the selected role's PermissionMatrix in a SectionCard (built-in: read-only with "Duplicate"; custom: editable, Cancel – Save in the card's header while there are changes), and a DataTable of invitations (e-mail, role, invited by, sent, expires, a state badge Pending (info) / Expired (warning), a row menu: Send again, Revoke). "Invite user" opens a Drawer from the end (D14: a short edit with the list visible): e-mail, role, Cancel – Send invitation. The three lists are page-level Tabs (start-aligned, D16); the drawer stands at page level outside the Tabs.
  - Messages: `permissions.cell`, `permissions.allowed`, `permissions.notAllowed`, `permissions.notApplicable`, `permissions.area`.

- **2026-10-08 — SetupChecklist (P5.21).** `SetupChecklist` (exported) with `setupProgress`, `resumeStep` (exported, tested). **One card** (raised, `border.default`, radius lg) so the highlighted step's band reaches its edges and the card ends with its last step: the title (h2 by default, the h4 size), an optional description, the progress "2 of 5 done" (`messages['setup.progress'](done, doneText, total, totalText)`) as xs medium text beside a ProgressBar (at most 240px, named by the same text). The steps in order, rows divided by `border.subtle` lines, 12px by 16px: a 24px marker — done: a check on `surface.sunken` in a `border.strong` ring (LifecycleBar's completed step, neutral); blocked: a Lock; open: its number (`format.number`) —, the title (sm semibold, with its state for assistive technology: `messages['setup.done' | 'setup.next' | 'setup.todo' | 'setup.blocked']`), the description, a done step's note (who, when) and a blocked step's reason in words. **Resume:** the step to do now — the application's `current`, else the first step still to do (a blocked step waits for something else) — is highlighted the neutral way (`surface.selected` with the 3px `border.selected` bar at its start, `data-liro-surface="selected"`, D17), carries `aria-current="step"`, and its action is the main button (primary, ArrowRight); other open steps' actions are small neutral buttons; a blocked step has none; a done step may offer "Change". Actions are links (`href`, through `linkComponent`, drawn as buttons with their colours named for every link state) or buttons (`onClick`). `loading`: skeleton rows. **Which to use:** SetupChecklist for a company's first-run steps; FormWizard for one multi-step form; Questionnaire (P5.1) for questions that lead to a document; LifecycleBar for the states of one document.
  - Messages: `setup.progress`, `setup.done`, `setup.next`, `setup.todo`, `setup.blocked`.

- **2026-10-08 — Signing: SignerList and SigningPage (P5.21).** The signing itself is done by the Core or Liro Bridge; the Design System shows who signs and reports the user's choice.
  1. **`SignerList`** (exported, with `signingSummary`, `signerTurn`, `maySign`, tested): the summary first, "1 of 3 signed" (`messages['signing.summary']`, a polite status that follows the changes); the signers in order (an ordered list, rows divided by lines): the order number in a 24px `border.strong` ring, the avatar, the name (sm semibold; "(you)" after the current user's, `messages['signing.you']`), the role (xs `text.secondary`), the state in words — "Waiting" (`text.secondary`), "Signed 05.10.2026. 14:12" with a check in `status.success.fg`, "Declined 06.10.2026. 09:02" with an X in `status.danger.fg` and the reason on its own line — never colour alone; the dates through `instantText`.
  2. **Whose turn:** in order (`sequential`, default) the first signer still waiting may sign, as long as nobody declined (a declined document stops); `sequential={false}`: any waiting signer. The current user's row, when it is their turn, has **Decline then Sign** — the main action last (D13): Decline is the destructive light button (X), Sign the primary button (PenLine), busy while the Core's promise runs (`aria-busy`). Decline opens a **ReasonConfirmDialog** (the Core's `declineReasons`, or a written reason; `declineMessage` says what happens; Cancel takes the focus, P4.9d). Before their turn the row says whose turn comes first: "Stefan Nikolić signs first" (`messages['signing.notYourTurn']`). Someone who does not sign sees only the states.
  3. **`SigningPage`** (template, exported) **is built on DetailPage** — one header, one frame, one side-column rule, not a layout of its own: the header (back, the document's number as the h1, its status, a subtitle, the actions), the **preview** section first and edge to edge (`preview`: the application's viewer — DocumentFrame of P5.5), the **attachments** section (`attachments`: an AttachmentList; left out without them), further `sections`, and the **signers** (`signers`: a SignerList) in a SectionCard in the 300px side column from 75em (under the content below). **Phones:** the signers come first — the person came to sign or to see who has —, then the document and the attachments. Section titles from `messages['signing.signers' | 'signing.document' | 'signing.attachments']` unless the application names them.
  - **Which list for what:** SignerList for the signatures a document needs; HistoryList (P5.1) for everything that happened to a record; StatusTimeline (P5.4) for a process's states with a next step; LifecycleBar for the dots above a document.
  - Messages: `signing.summary`, `signing.waiting`, `signing.signedAt`, `signing.declinedAt`, `signing.you`, `signing.sign`, `signing.decline`, `signing.declineTitle`, `signing.declineConfirm`, `signing.notYourTurn`, `signing.signers`, `signing.document`, `signing.attachments`.
